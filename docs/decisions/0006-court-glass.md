# 0006: Court glass walls

**Date:** 2026-11-16 · **Status:** proposed (court glass agent)

- **One mesh, `SM_CourtGlass`.** All 18 panels (5 per back wall, a full-height and a step panel per side per half) plus their bolt fixings, built by `Tools/AssetPipeline/blender/build_court_glass.py`. The venue places it at the origin. The fixings are opaque sections of the same mesh, so there's one path to place. It isn't Nanite (Nanite can't render translucent materials) and is about 25k triangles, mostly bolts.
- **Sizes come from `CourtSpec.h`.** Panel heights and positions are read from the header (GDD 2), and the inner faces sit exactly on the playing-area limits, like the colliders in `CourtLayout.h`. The build details that aren't regulation numbers are in `Tools/AssetPipeline/config/glass.json`: 12 mm tempered glass, 8 mm joints centred on the post lines (every 2 m), a 1.5 mm arris on every edge and 8 mm dubbed corners. The layout is in `asset_lib/glass.py`, which is plain Python, so `tests/test_glass.py` checks it without Blender.
- **Fixings.** Panels hang on the frame's posts (100 mm across the joint, 50 mm deep, starting 3 mm outside the glass, agreed with the frame agent). Each vertical edge has M12 countersunk through-bolts, 34 mm from the edge, at 0.25, 1.5 and 2.75 m on the 3 m panels and 0.25 and 1.75 m on the 2 m step. The court side is flush, apart from a stainless head 0.6 mm proud inside a black nylon bush, because nothing may stick out into play. Behind the glass are an EPDM washer, the post, a steel washer and the nut. The glass config refuses holes closer to the edge than twice the thickness, the tempered-glass drilling rule.
- **Material (`M_PadelGlass`, `unreal/glass_material.py`).** Translucent with Surface ForwardShading, so it gets real specular and reflections. Opacity is about 5 % head-on, so the ball always reads (GDD 10). At grazing angles Fresnel makes it more reflective, more opaque and greener. The edges use the same master as `MI_Court_GlassEdge`, which is dense green, the iron tint of soda-lime glass. Three small procedural textures from `asset_lib/glass.py` add the dirt: smears and cloth wipes that blur reflections, felt ball marks in the band of heights where balls hit, and sand dust that fades up from the floor. A faint roller-wave normal map bends reflections the way real tempered panels do. Every panel samples a different window of the textures. All values are in `config/palette.json` and `config/glass.json`.
- **Performance.** There are 18 low-poly translucent panels with 3 texture samples each. The material has no refraction (10 to 12 mm of flat glass barely shifts the image), no two-sided lighting and no depth writes. The glass is two translucent layers deep (two faces) wherever the camera looks through a wall.
- **Needs the venue.** Mirror-like reflections need Lumen front-layer translucency reflections turned on in the project (`r.Lumen.TranslucencyReflections.FrontLayer.Enable=1`) or reflection captures around the court. That belongs to the lighting and venue work. The dust and ball-mark heights use world Z, so the court floor must stay at Z = 0.
- **Later.** GDD 2's random bounces off the joints between panels, and per-match dirt that builds up where balls actually hit (a decal or render-target layer driven by `OnBallBounced`).
