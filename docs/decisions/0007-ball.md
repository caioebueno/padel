# 0007: The ball, and how it reads in motion

**Status:** proposed (ball art). **GDD:** 2 (ball 65 mm, 57 g, lower pressure than a tennis ball), 10 (bright yellow, the brightest thing on screen, a subtle motion trail at high speed, the seam shows spin), 12.

## Decision

- **Built by the pipeline, like the court parts.** `SM_Ball` comes from `Tools/AssetPipeline/blender/build_equipment.py` with `config/ball.json`; colours are in `palette.json` `ball`. The size is GDD 2's 65 mm. Pressure doesn't change how the ball looks, so it lives only in the physics (`Data/Physics.json`).
- **Hand-built LODs, not Nanite.** The ball is tiny, moves every frame and its LOD0 has a masked shell, so it's a plain static mesh with three LODs that are each a UV sphere built for that size: LOD0 (64 × 32 segments plus the fuzz shell, about 6,200 triangles) for close-ups and replays, LOD1 (960) and LOD2 (288) for gameplay. From the broadcast camera the ball is about ten pixels across, so the game nearly always draws LOD2. Building each LOD keeps the equirectangular UVs exact, which decimation would break.
- **The detail is in the material, from four procedural textures** (`asset_lib/ball_textures.py`, seeded, standard library only):
  - `T_Ball_Masks` (equirectangular, uncompressed): R is the **distance from the seam's centre line**, G is slow dye and nap mottle, B is the printed mark. The seam is the classic two-lobe curve, so the two halves are equal dog-bone shapes.
  - `T_Ball_N`: the seam's rubber groove (0.35 mm deep) and the felt's soft shoulder where it is glued down.
  - `T_Felt_N` and `T_Felt_Mask`: one tile of curly felt fibres, repeated about every 9 mm and faded out beyond 4 m, where it would only shimmer.
- **Felt is Cloth shading.** `M_PadelBallFelt` uses Unreal's Cloth shading model with a pale fuzz colour, which gives felt's soft sheen at grazing angles. The rubber seam turns the cloth term off and is smoother. LOD0 adds a **fuzz shell** 0.6 mm outside the ball, with a masked material drawing loose fibres, denser towards the outline, so a close-up ball has a soft edge instead of a billiard-ball edge.
- **The seam stays readable at any distance.** The material cuts the seam from the distance field with a one-pixel filter, and never lets it get thinner than `min_seam_pixels` on screen (1.4 px). Up close it is the real 3 mm. From the broadcast camera it becomes a 1 to 2 pixel stripe that visibly turns with the spin, instead of fading into the felt.
- **Spin is shown, not reproduced.** `APadelBall` turns the mesh about the simulation's own spin axis (`FBallView::Spin`, added for this), in the same direction. A smash spins at 50 turns a second or more, which is most of a turn per frame and reads as random jumps or even backwards rotation. So the shown rate is capped at `MaxShownRadPerFrame` (0.9 rad, about a seventh of a turn) per frame. Slow spin (a sliced lob, a chiquita) is shown exactly. The cap is per frame, not per second, because the aliasing is per frame.
- **The trail follows the path.** Above 15 m/s, `SM_BallTrail` (a tapered tube) is stretched from the ball back to where it was 40 ms ago, fading in to full by 30 m/s. Using the path rather than the velocity means that after a bounce off the glass the trail points back towards the glass, not through it. A jump of more than 1.5 m in one frame (a serve toss) starts a new trail instead of drawing a streak across the court. The trail is translucent and unlit, its opacity comes through custom primitive data (no dynamic material instances), and it casts no shadow.
- **Motion blur is the scene's.** The ball takes the venue's post-process motion blur like everything else (`Data/Lighting.json`, amount 0.25 and at most 2 % of the screen), which at smash speed smears it by about one ball width from the broadcast camera. That is what a TV picture does. The trail gives the direction, and the ball's colour and minimum brightness keep it readable.
- **Brightness.** The felt's albedo is about 0.8, so under the venue's 1,000 lux it is already the brightest surface on court. `readability_lift` adds a little felt-coloured light *relative to the exposure* (`EyeAdaptationInverse`), so the ball keeps a minimum on-screen brightness in a player's shadow without looking lit from inside.
- **An original mark, no text.** Each half carries a small printed court seen from above (outline, net line and a dot for the ball). No brand, no lettering.
- **The match and practice use it.** `APadelMatchDirector` and `APadelPracticeDirector` used to draw the engine sphere scaled to 6.5 cm. They now spawn `APadelBall` and call `Present()` each frame. Until `SM_Ball` is imported, `APadelBall` shows the engine sphere at 6.5 cm and logs once, so a fresh checkout still plays.

## Tuning

- Look: `Tools/AssetPipeline/config/ball.json` (seam, felt, mark, LOD segments) and `palette.json` `ball` (colours, roughness, lift, minimum seam width, fuzz density, trail colour). Re-run `pipeline.py all`.
- Motion: `Data/Ball.json` `Visual` (spin cap, trail speeds, length, opacity). Read at run time.
- `pipeline.py preview-ball out.png` renders a close-up in Blender with the same textures, to judge the felt, the seam and the mark without Unreal.

## Not done yet

- Scuffs and dirt from play: every ball is new.
- A ball cam for replays (GDD 9, post-slice) would be the first place LOD0 is seen often.
- The practice ball models no spin, so in practice the ball doesn't turn.
